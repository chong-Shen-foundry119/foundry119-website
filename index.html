Abstract

AI can draft a thousand hypotheses before lunch. A lab bench still tests them one shift at a time. Foundry 119 is building FoundryOS, which runs a lab's own procedures overnight, autonomously and verified, on standard lab instruments and forward-deployed robotics. FoundryOS has four parts: (i) an AI copilot, (ii) a digital twin of the bench, (iii) verified robotics, and (iv) an on-prem server.

On the name

The periodic table has 118 known elements. Element 119 would open a new row. Theory has described it for decades. Nobody has made it. It is waiting on an experiment.

1

The week

A lab that works one shift runs 40 of the week's 168 hours (Fig. 1). That is 24%. The other 128 hours are nights and weekends, with the instruments switched off.

The goal we are building toward is all 168 hours running. We do not claim it yet. Our first public benchmark run comes this winter.

Three things we have come to believe

2.1

Experiments are the scarce input.

Models keep getting better at proposing what to try. Someone still has to run it, measure it and trust the result. The bench decides how fast science moves.

2.2

Long runs fail on recovery.

A robot that gets each step right 99% of the time sounds reliable. Chain enough steps and almost nothing finishes (Fig. 2). So we check every step and recover.

2.3

A lab's science should stay its own.

Experimental capacity is concentrating in a few very large labs. We think every other lab should have its own loop, with its data in its own building and its models in its own name.

3

What we are building

FoundryOS installs in the lab you already have. It is one system with four parts. A lab can start with the software and add the robotics when a procedure is worth automating.

Table 1FoundryOS, one system in four parts.

A copilot
Scientists talk to it at any hour, including the one when the idea arrives.
A twin
A digital copy of the bench, where every run is rehearsed before reagents are spent.
Verified robotics
They run your own procedures on the equipment you already own, with a check at every step.
Your walls
It runs on a server the lab owns. Client work and data never leave the building.
